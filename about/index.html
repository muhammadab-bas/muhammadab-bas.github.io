About Me

A botany graduate from Swat, known as the Switzerland of Pakistan, building a research path at the intersection of plant function, resilience, and health.

Hello, I’m Abbas!

I am a botany graduate from Swat, Pakistan, interested in how plants respond to environmental stress and disease. My research experience began in plant stress physiology, and I am developing interests in plant health and the molecular mechanisms underlying plant stress responses.

I completed my Bachelor of Science in Botany at the University of Swat, Pakistan, during 2021–2025, graduating with a CGPA of 4.0/4.0 and the distinction of gold medalist.

For my undergraduate thesis, supervised by Dr. Ahmad Ali, I examined the effects of proline seed priming on salt-stress tolerance in wild and cultivated lentils. Through this project, I gained experience in research design and methodology, measuring plant responses, and statistical analysis.

I am currently working on a systematic review and meta-analysis of trunk-injected oxytetracycline for citrus Huanglongbing, also known as citrus greening, through a meta-analysis mentorship at Multiomics. This project examines published evidence on disease management and has broadened my interest in plant pathology and evidence synthesis.

These experiences helped shape my interests in plant stress physiology, pathology, plant–microbe interactions, molecular genetics, and crop improvement. I am especially interested in research that connects mechanisms to practical problems in plant health and sustainable agriculture.

I hope to pursue a research-based Master’s and, in time, a PhD. My long-term goal is to become a research scientist and educator who contributes to resilient crop systems and supports future learners in Pakistan and beyond.

Communication as a Research Skill

Teaching and tutoring have shaped how I communicate as much as my lab work has shaped how I think. Explaining a difficult idea clearly, listening carefully, and remaining open to revising an explanation are habits I consider essential to collaborative research, and ones I am committed to carrying forward.
