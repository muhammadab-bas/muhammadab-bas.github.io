Hello, I’m Abbas

My academic journey has been driven by a simple but persistent question: how do plants, rooted in one place for their entire lives, perceive and survive stress, and how can that knowledge be used to build healthier and more resilient crops? This question fascinated me and eventually led me to pursue a B.S. in Botany at the University of Swat, where I graduated with distinction as a gold medalist.

In July 2024, I joined the Plant Physiology Lab where I started working on my final-year project to understand whether priming lentil seeds with proline, a natural stress-protective compound, could help them survive salt stress before emergence. Working across petri dish and pot experiments, I tracked how primed and unprimed genotypes responded at every level, germination speed, chlorophyll retention, proline accumulation, antioxidant enzyme activity, and even the architecture of their roots as they pushed downward in search of stability. This work revealed how a single priming treatment can reprogram the plant’s stress response from the first hours of germination to its physiological resilience after weeks.

These experiences sparked a new and more specific interest: how plants defend themselves against pests and pathogens. That curiosity is what currently drives my work on a systematic review examining oxytetracycline trunk injection for citrus Huanglongbing, a devastating bacterial disease reshaping citrus agriculture worldwide. This work has deepened my interest in plant pathology and plant-microbe interactions, and in how evidence-based research can guide real solutions for crop protection.

I am currently seeking an MS/PhD position in plant stress physiology, pathology, and molecular genetics, where questions of stress tolerance and disease resistance are addressed to develop climate‑smart, sustainable agricultural solutions.
